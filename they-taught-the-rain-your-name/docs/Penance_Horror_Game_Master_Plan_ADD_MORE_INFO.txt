DEMO DESIGN SUMMARY
================================================================================

Game Type
---------
First-person horror demo set in a looping, liminal suburban neighborhood during
heavy rain at night.

Core Fantasy
------------
The player arrives in a neighborhood that looks physically ordinary but behaves
like it is remembering guilt. Roads loop. Doors appear. Houses repeat. Lights
lead and betray the player. Penance is visible before he is fully understood.

Current Demo Goal
-----------------
Build a playable vertical slice where the player can:

1. Arrive on a dark suburban street.
2. Follow soft guidance toward the first lit house.
3. Notice signs that the neighborhood is wrong.
4. Experience a first Penance sighting.
5. Move through the neighborhood loop, park, cul-de-sac, church/community center,
   and storm-drain/basement path.
6. Understand that the final house was visible the whole time but unreachable.
7. Unlock the final house route later through a scripted event.

This demo should prove:

- Scale
- Navigation
- Sightlines
- Mood
- Event timing
- Penance presence
- Environmental storytelling
- Basic player readability

This demo should NOT try to prove:

- Final art quality
- Final combat/chase complexity
- Full inventory depth
- Full dialogue/cutscene system
- Final Penance model polish
- Full neighborhood lore database

LOCKED STORY CANON FROM FOSTER HOUSE ORIGIN LORE
================================================================================

Source document:

    res://docs/Penance_Foster_House_Origin_Lore_EXPLICIT.txt

Important handling rule:

    This lore is explicit for the developer/writer, but the demo should present it
    with restraint. Do not show the assault. Do not use the sister's suffering as
    shock content. The demo should communicate cover-up, control, silence,
    erased evidence, and guilt through environment, documents, sound, and staging.

Core Story
----------
The player returns to a neighborhood controlled by a church that helped cover up
a violent crime against his sister. The Foster House was presented as a place of
care, shelter, discipline, and religious guidance, but it became the center of
the cover-up.

The sister was isolated there after the crime. The church protected reputation
and power instead of protecting her. The child connected to the cover-up became
living evidence, then the sister died and the child vanished from records.

The horror is not random evil.

The horror is:

- institutional protection
- community silence
- shame used as control
- truth buried under religious language
- the player realizing he escaped while his sister did not

One-line theme:

    The worst thing was not only what happened to her. It was that everyone let
    it happen.

Player Identity
---------------
The player is the victim's brother.

He left the neighborhood years ago. He knew something was wrong but did not
understand the full truth. He heard arguments, saw whispering, saw the church get
more involved, and saw his sister withdraw. He escaped. She did not.

Player emotional conflict:

- He wants answers.
- He feels guilty for leaving.
- He fears he ignored signs.
- He slowly realizes the town was haunted by what its own people did.

Sister Identity
---------------
The sister is the emotional center of the story.

Do not reduce her to a plot device. The Foster House should eventually reveal who
she was before the institution reduced her to scandal and evidence.

Demo storytelling rule:

    The first demo should not reveal everything, but it must make the player feel
    that a real person was erased, not that the game is chasing abstract lore.

Foster House Identity
---------------------
The "final house" is now canonically the Foster House.

It should remain visible from multiple angles throughout the demo. Early on it is
unreachable. Later, after the church/basement path, the road remembers it and the
player can finally approach.

The Foster House represents false safety:

- a home that was a cage
- care used as control
- shelter used as isolation
- religious language used to hide evidence

Penance Identity
----------------
Penance is the manifestation of guilt, silence, and punishment.

He is not a random killer. He is the shape the buried sin takes.

Penance exists to force people to face what they allowed.

He represents:

- the sister's erased suffering
- the child's erased existence
- the church's hypocrisy
- the community's silence
- the player's guilt for leaving
- the truth hidden inside the Foster House

Penance behavior rule:

    Penance is dragging the player through every place where the truth was hidden.

Silent Child Implication
------------------------
There should be a quiet implication that Penance may be the erased child.

Do not state this outright early.
Do not have a character explain it.
Do not put a note in the demo that says "Penance is the child."

The implication should come from absence:

- No official birth record survives.
- The child has no confirmed name.
- The child has no grave.
- Photos have missing faces or empty spaces.
- The Foster House has child evidence but no complete file.
- Penance is always connected to missing records, locked rooms, old photos, and
  the Foster House.
- Penance watches from the Foster House as if he belongs there.
- Penance feels both like punishment and like proof that the erased life still
  exists somewhere.

Design rule:

    Penance should be readable as guilt made physical, but players should slowly
    suspect that the guilt has a more specific shape: the child no record can
    prove existed.

This is stronger as subtext than exposition. The player should ask:

    "Is Penance what the town created, or is he what the town erased?"

Church Identity
---------------
The church is not just a spooky building.

It is the institution that protected itself instead of protecting the victim.

When Penance opens the church door, it means:

    The truth starts here.

Basement / Storm-Drain Identity
-------------------------------
The basement and storm-drain path represent buried truth.

Going underground means going below the neighborhood's public story. For the
demo, the first underground sequence should be survival and pressure, not a full
explanation of the entire crime.

Gun Identity
------------
The gun should not turn the game into an action game.

The gun represents temporary control. It can stagger Penance, damage the body, or
knock off cloth/wood/visual pieces, but it cannot kill what Penance represents.

For the first demo, the gun can be teased or saved for a later demo cut if scope
gets too large. If included, it must feel limited and desperate.

BORDERLINE AAA DEMO TARGET
================================================================================

AAA-Level Meaning for This Project
----------------------------------
"Borderline AAA" does not mean huge scope.

For this demo, it means:

- one polished playable route
- strong first 10-20 minutes
- readable objectives without obvious arrows
- excellent lighting composition
- strong sound design
- reliable triggers
- no softlocks
- no confusing dead ends
- high-quality pacing
- one unforgettable Penance reveal
- one controlled pressure/chase sequence
- one clean ending hook

The demo should feel expensive because it is focused, reliable, and staged well,
not because it has too many features.

Recommended Demo Scope
----------------------
Target length:

    15-25 minutes for a first-time player.

Primary route:

    Arrival Street
        ->
    First Lit House
        ->
    Penance Window / Silhouette
        ->
    Neighborhood Loop Shift
        ->
    Park Lure
        ->
    Cul-de-sac Trap
        ->
    Church Opens
        ->
    Basement / Storm-Drain Pressure Sequence
        ->
    Foster House Reveal / Demo Ending

Optional route complexity:

    Very low. The player can explore a little, but the demo should be tightly
    controlled. A strong horror demo is better than a loose map.

AAA Demo Pillars
----------------
1. Cinematic Environmental Staging
   - Every major area should have a clear composition.
   - Use lighting, silhouettes, rain, reflections, and framing.
   - The player should naturally look where the game needs them to look.

2. Premium Audio Direction
   - Rain is constant but layered.
   - Important cues cut through rain clearly.
   - Silence is used intentionally before major beats.
   - Penance has a recognizable chain/cloth/low-presence sound identity.

3. Reliable Scripted Horror
   - Scripted events should feel authored, not random.
   - Every major scare should have setup, reveal, and aftermath.
   - Avoid cheap repeated jumpscares.

4. Strong Player Readability
   - The world can be confusing emotionally, but navigation must be readable.
   - If the player is lost, it should be for a designed moment, not because the
     level lacks guidance.

5. Restraint With Sensitive Lore
   - The demo should imply enough to be disturbing and meaningful.
   - Save the most explicit truth for later Foster House discovery.
   - Never make the crime itself spectacle.

6. Technical Cleanliness
   - No visible broken triggers.
   - No debug lighting in final demo mode.
   - No player snagging on blockout geometry.
   - No Penance spawning in silly or accidental positions.
   - Restart/failure flow must work.

Demo Ending Target
------------------
Recommended first demo ending:

    Player exits or survives the underground pressure sequence.
    A church monitor, security TV, or rain-glitched screen shows the Foster House.
    Penance is standing inside the Foster House in the same silhouette language
    as the first lit-house sighting.
    The monitor glitches.
    Outside, the Foster House becomes visually dominant and reachable or almost
    reachable.
    Penance turns slightly toward the camera.
    Cut to black.

Title card:

    PENANCE
    This was only the first descent.

This ending gives the player a clear completion point while preserving mystery.

CURRENT BLOCKOUT MAP PLAN
================================================================================

Scale Rule
----------
Use 1 Godot unit = 1 meter.

Current Coordinate Logic
------------------------
The current blockout is built around an approximate 150m x 180m playable area.

Important areas:

- Arrival street:
  Starts near the player spawn and leads into the loop.

- Main road loop:
  A rectangular suburban loop that lets the player circle the neighborhood.

- First enterable house:
  Located near the arrival/main loop connection.
  This should become the first meaningful objective.

- Park:
  Off the loop, with wet grass, path, playset, swing, bench, trees, and a storm
  drain entrance.

- Cul-de-sac:
  A side loop/terminal space that should feel like a trap and a transition point.

- Church/community center:
  A larger landmark visible from the road and tied to bells, punishment, and
  public guilt.

- Storm-drain/basement route:
  Underground path intended for chase pacing and forced progression.

- Foster House / Final House:
  Visible from several angles, blocked early, reachable later. This is the real
  center of the cover-up, not just a spooky destination.

Navigation Design Rules
-----------------------
- The player should always have at least one visible landmark.
- The Foster House should be visible before it is reachable.
- The church should be visible above or through neighborhood silhouettes.
- The first lit house should be the most inviting thing early.
- The park should be found through sound and motion, not UI.
- The cul-de-sac should feel optional at first, then become unavoidable.
- The storm drain should feel like a bad idea but also the only forward path.

Sightline Rules
---------------
- Keep long views down the main road loop.
- Use fences, parked cars, trees, and house corners to create partial occlusion.
- Penance should appear at the end of long views only during controlled moments.
- Avoid placing too many large blockers in the road loop until the scale feels
  correct.
- Do not overfill the blockout with detail before playtesting walking routes.

CURRENT DEMO OBJECTIVE FLOW
================================================================================

Phase 0 — Spawn / Arrival
-------------------------
Player starts on the arrival street in rain.

Desired player thought:

    "I need to move toward the neighborhood."

Implementation needs:

- Spawn facing the loop.
- Add an abandoned car or road closure behind the player.
- Add rain, fog, weak streetlights, and wet-road shine.
- Keep debug lighting available during development, but tune horror lighting with
  debug mode off.

Phase 1 — First Lit House
-------------------------
One house should be warmer and more readable than the others.

Desired player thought:

    "That house is probably where I should go."

Implementation needs:

- Porch/window light.
- Light spill on wet sidewalk.
- Front path clearly readable.
- Window framing for the first Penance silhouette.
- Interior clue that triggers the next neighborhood change.

Phase 2 — First Penance Sighting
--------------------------------
Penance appears as a silhouette inside or beyond the first lit house.

Desired player thought:

    "Was someone standing there?"

Implementation needs:

- Do not reveal full monster detail.
- Use backlight, not front light.
- Silhouette should vanish when the player changes angle or after a short timer.
- Add quiet audio: low hum, rain muffling, one chain creak.
- Do not start a chase here.

Phase 3 — Neighborhood Loop Becomes Wrong
-----------------------------------------
After the first clue, roads should start misbehaving.

Desired player thought:

    "I came from here, but this is not the same."

Implementation needs:

- One road loops the player back before final-house unlock.
- Streetlights flicker in the wrong sequence.
- A door or shrine appears where there was a wall/fence.
- The final house is visible in the distance during lightning.

Phase 4 — Park Lure
-------------------
The park should pull the player through sound and motion.

Desired player thought:

    "Something moved over there."

Implementation needs:

- Swing movement or creak.
- Flickering park lamp.
- Wet bench/shrine clue.
- Storm-drain opening visible but not immediately inviting.
- Optional environmental clue connecting children/family/community silence to
  the neighborhood guilt.

Phase 5 — Cul-de-sac Trap
-------------------------
The cul-de-sac should feel like a dead end that becomes a ritual space.

Desired player thought:

    "This road should go somewhere, but it does not."

Implementation needs:

- Repeated facades or identical doors.
- Car parked at wrong angle.
- Light flicker that briefly shows Penance at a distance.
- Bell or church sound that redirects player.
- Road behind player subtly changes or darkens.

Phase 6 — Church / Community Center
-----------------------------------
The church is not only religious. It should also feel like the neighborhood's
public excuse machine: meetings, silence, punishment, missing photos, old rules.

Desired player thought:

    "This place knows what happened."

Implementation needs:

- Door opens after cul-de-sac event.
- Bell tower as landmark.
- Notice board with old photos.
- Candles and rotten wood at threshold.
- Basement/storm-drain entrance.
- Clue that frames Penance as judgment, not just a monster.

Phase 7 — Basement / Storm-Drain Chase Path
-------------------------------------------
The underground path should be a controlled fear sequence.

Desired player thought:

    "I need to keep moving."

Implementation needs:

- Narrow path, turns, water channel, rusted pipes.
- Penance audio behind player before visual contact.
- One forced look-forward moment, not constant backtracking.
- A clear exit silhouette.
- Do not overcomplicate AI here yet. A scripted chase is enough for the demo.

Phase 8 — Foster House Becomes Reachable
----------------------------------------
After the tunnel event, the road to the Foster House appears/unlocks.

Desired player thought:

    "That house was always there. Now I can reach it."

Implementation needs:

- Gate/barrier disappears or opens.
- Road extension becomes visible.
- Penance silhouette relocates closer to the final house.
- Foster House light changes from distant background to active objective.
- Demo can end at the front door or inside the first room, depending on scope.

CHARACTER PLAN
================================================================================

The Player / Visitor
--------------------
Current role:

    The player is an outsider or returning figure entering a neighborhood that
    should not be accessible anymore.

Design purpose:

- The player gives the audience a reason to learn the place from zero.
- The player should not fully understand Penance at the start.
- The player's ignorance lets environmental storytelling do most of the work.

Open character question:

    Is the player a true outsider, a former resident, a family member, or someone
    connected to the original wrong?

Recommendation for demo:

    Keep the player mostly undefined. Let the demo focus on place, dread, and
    Penance. Add only one clue implying the player may have been expected.

Possible clue:

    A photo in the first house has the player's face scratched out or left blank.

Penance
-------
Current role:

    Penance is the main monster and symbolic judge of the neighborhood.

Core visual identity:

- Tall hooded figure.
- Crushed door-like mask.
- Hanging wet cloth.
- Rusted chains.
- Rotten wood.
- Candles/photos/relics.
- Punishment and religious/community-guilt imagery.
- Water stains and rain-warped materials.

Core behavior identity:

    Penance should feel less like an animal predator and more like a sentence
    being carried out.

He should:

- Watch before attacking.
- Appear where guilt is concentrated.
- Use doors, bells, roads, and light as signals.
- Be associated with judgment, locked rooms, hidden basements, and public silence.
- Feel slow and inevitable until the chase requires speed.

He should NOT:

- Behave like a random zombie.
- Scream constantly.
- Chase too early.
- Appear so often that he becomes normal.
- Explain himself through dialogue.

Penance Presence Modes
----------------------
Use three modes for the demo:

1. Witness Mode
   - Far silhouette.
   - Window figure.
   - Lightning reveal.
   - No chase.

2. Pressure Mode
   - Audio behind player.
   - Door/chain events.
   - Streetlights fail.
   - Player knows he is near but cannot always see him.

3. Sentence Mode
   - Scripted chase.
   - Used underground or near the final route.
   - Clear forward path.
   - Punishes hesitation, not exploration.

Church / Community Figures
--------------------------
These do not need full character models yet.

Use them as environmental characters:

- The Pastor / Community Leader
  Represents public denial, punishment language, and moral cover.

- The Missing Family
  Represents the private tragedy that the neighborhood ignored.

- The Neighbors
  Represent silence, watching through curtains, photos turned around, and doors
  locked from the outside.

- The Children / Park Memory
  Optional. Use carefully. The park can imply family/community harm without
  needing explicit shock content.

Implementation recommendation:

    For the demo, represent these characters through photos, notices, signs,
    chairs, doors, candles, and audio fragments. Do not build NPCs yet.

================================================================================
DEMO SUCCESS CRITERIA
================================================================================

The demo is working when a new player can:

- Start the project and immediately understand they should move forward.
- Notice the first lit house without being told.
- Feel unsafe entering the neighborhood.
- See or sense Penance before understanding him.
- Navigate the loop without getting permanently lost.
- Understand that the park/cul-de-sac/church are important.
- Reach the tunnel/basement path.
- Experience one controlled pressure/chase moment.
- Understand that the final house was the destination.
- Finish the demo wanting to know what happened there.

The demo is failing if:

- The player wanders for more than a few minutes with no idea where to go.
- The world is too dark to read.
- The route only works because the developer explains it.
- Penance appears too much and stops being scary.
- The final house feels random instead of inevitable.
- The player gets stuck on blockout collision.
- The church feels unrelated to Penance.
- The tunnel feels like filler instead of escalation.

================================================================================
LOCKED ANSWERS FOR CURRENT DEMO FLOW
================================================================================

These answers come from the Foster House origin lore and should guide the demo.

1. Who is the player?

    The player is the victim's brother. He left the neighborhood years ago and
    carries guilt because he sensed something was wrong but escaped before he
    understood the full truth.

2. What happened in the neighborhood?

    A powerful man connected to the Foster House harmed the player's sister, and
    the church/community protected reputation and authority instead of protecting
    her. She was isolated in the Foster House, the child became hidden evidence,
    and records were erased.

3. What is Penance enforcing?

    Penance enforces memory, confession, and punishment. He is not trying to kill
    randomly. He forces the player through places where the truth was hidden.
    There is also a silent implication that Penance may be the erased child,
    because the child vanished from all records and Penance is repeatedly tied to
    missing proof, child evidence, and the Foster House. Keep this as subtext, not
    a confirmed demo reveal.

4. What is inside the Foster House?

    The Foster House contains the emotional truth:

    - Sister's room
    - Birth room
    - Decision room
    - Child evidence area
    - documents/photos/proof that the community erased what happened

    The first demo can point toward these rooms without fully exploring them.

5. How should the demo end?

    The demo should end by revealing that the Foster House is the real center of
    the story. Use a monitor/TV/security-screen reveal or direct sightline reveal,
    show Penance inside or aligned with the Foster House, then cut to black with a
    clear title card.

6. What should remain mysterious?

    The demo should not fully explain every detail. It should make the player
    understand that:

    - the church hid something
    - the Foster House is the center
    - Penance is connected to buried guilt
    - the player's sister matters
    - the player has personal guilt

    The exact documents, names, timeline, and child evidence can be saved for the
    full Foster House sequence.


Idea - Player feels like the hero… but slowly realizes they weren’t innocent.

That gives you:
- motivation (hero energy)
- AND horror (guilt / unease)

STEP 1 — LOCK PLAYER EXPERIENCE (MOST IMPORTANT)
================================================================================

Before adding detail, make sure:

- Movement feels good.
  - Walk speed feels natural.
  - Sprint feels responsive.
  - Camera movement feels smooth and not annoying.

- Flashlight works.
  - Range feels useful but not overpowered.
  - Flicker feels scary, not broken.
  - Battery system can be added if wanted, but do not overcomplicate it early.

- Collisions feel right.
  - No weird clipping.
  - Player does not get caught on small objects.
  - Doors, stairs, sidewalks, curbs, and tight hallways feel playable.

- Scale feels correct.
  - Doors should feel like real doors.
  - Streets should feel believable.
  - Rooms should not feel too huge or too tiny.
  - Church, basement, and neighborhood spaces should feel physically grounded.

If this feels off, EVERYTHING after feels bad.

Purpose
-------
This document organizes the full objective-path guidance system and the specific
cul-de-sac/church/Penance setpiece into one clean plan for Godot.

Core Design Goal
----------------
The player should feel lost, but they should actually be quietly guided forward
the whole time.

The game should NOT feel like random wandering. It should feel confusing,
unsettling, and scary, but still intentionally designed.

Main Rule
---------
At any moment, the player should subconsciously think:

    "I think I should go there..."

Even if the game never explicitly says where to go.

================================================================================
1. MAIN OBJECTIVE PATH DESIGN
================================================================================

Best System for This Game
-------------------------
Use this combo:

1. Soft Guidance
   - Primary method.
   - Guides the player without arrows, UI, or obvious objective markers.

2. Gated Progression
   - Secondary method.
   - Controls the order of events by locking/unlocking paths, doors, and areas.

3. Forced Redirection
   - Used for horror moments.
   - Manipulates the environment so the player feels lost while still being moved
     toward the intended path.

================================================================================
2. SOFT GUIDANCE
================================================================================

Soft guidance means the environment quietly tells the player where to go.
Do not rely on arrows or UI.

Use these tools:

A. Light = Direction
--------------------
Light tells the player what matters.

Examples:
- Brighter area = "go here"
- Flickering light = "important"
- Total darkness = "not yet"
- Only one house has a porch light on = player naturally goes there

B. Motion = Attention
---------------------
Humans notice movement first.

Use movement to pull the player's eyes toward important areas.

Examples:
- Swinging object
- Flickering TV
- Moving curtain
- Blinking light

C. Sound = Invisible Arrow
--------------------------
Sound can guide the player without visual UI.

Examples:
- Whisper from a direction
- Chain dragging
- TV static
- Crying
- Wind

The player naturally follows sound.

D. Contrast = Importance
------------------------
Make important things stand out from everything else.

Examples:
- Everything dark -> one lit window
- Everything quiet -> one noisy area
- Everything looks the same -> one different object

E. Landmarking
--------------
Always show important locations from multiple angles so the player builds a
mental map.

Possible landmarks:
- Final house visible from far away
- Church visible over rooftops
- Red light in the distance

The player should always have a mental target, even if they do not fully
understand it yet.

================================================================================
3. GATED PROGRESSION
================================================================================

Gated progression controls flow.

You do not tell the player "go here."
Instead, you make the correct direction the only path that works.

Examples:
- Locked door -> player needs a key or note
- Blocked road -> fallen tree prevents progress
- Locked fence -> opens after a trigger
- Door appears only after an event
- Basement opens after church event

Simple Progression Formula
--------------------------

    Explore -> Find item -> Trigger event -> Unlock next area

This keeps the game structured without feeling like a checklist.

================================================================================
4. FORCED REDIRECTION
================================================================================

Forced redirection is used for horror control.
This is where the game becomes scary because the world starts manipulating the
player.

Examples:
- Player walks forward -> ends up back where they started
- Player exits a house -> appears in a new location
- Road loops into cul-de-sac
- Door disappears behind them
- Map changes after they turn around

Purpose:
- The player feels confused.
- The designer still controls where the player goes.
- The environment becomes part of the horror.

================================================================================
5. IDEAL FULL GAME PATH
================================================================================

Clean Structure
---------------

    Arrival Street
        ↓
    Lit House
    Only visible light source.
        ↓
    Clue Found
    Triggers neighborhood change.
        ↓
    Park
    Sound + movement attracts player.
        ↓

        ↓

================================================================================
5A. FIRST LIT-HOUSE PENANCE SILHOUETTE SCENE — EXPANDED EXPLANATION
================================================================================

Core Purpose
------------
This is the player's first real visual encounter with Penance, but it should NOT
feel like a full monster reveal yet.

The goal is not "big jumpscare." The goal is uncertainty.

The player should go through this exact emotional chain:

    "Wait... someone's inside."
        ↓
    "Is that a person?"
        ↓
    "Why are they standing like that?"
        ↓
    "That is not human."
        ↓
    "What the fuck was that?"
        ↓
    "Where did it go?"

This moment works because the player is not immediately told what they saw.
They are forced to question it.

Design Rule
-----------
Make Penance look like a person at first glance and a mistake at second glance.

Use this ratio:

    80% person-shaped
    20% deeply wrong

If he looks too monstrous immediately, the mystery disappears.
If he looks too normal, the fear disappears.

He should sit right in the uncomfortable middle.

Why This Scene Belongs in the Lit House
---------------------------------------
The lit house is already the player's first soft-guidance objective.
It is the only warm, visible, inviting location in the dark neighborhood.

That makes the moment stronger because the player naturally thinks:

    "That house is probably safe."

Then the silhouette corrupts that assumption.

The house light becomes bait.
The player is not just finding shelter. They are being pulled into the story.

In-Story Reason Penance Is There
--------------------------------
Penance is not randomly wandering around the neighborhood.
He is guiding, judging, and luring the player.

The best canon explanation:

    The lit house is connected to the first place where the neighborhood ignored
    something terrible. It represents the beginning of the silence.

Penance appears there because that house is one of the first places where the
neighborhood's guilt leaks through.

He is not there to kill the player yet.
He is there to make the player witness.

At this stage, Penance is:

- Warning the player
- Testing the player
- Pulling the player deeper
- Asking whether the player will also look away

Short lore version:

    Penance appears in the lit house because the light is a lure and the house
    marks the first place the neighborhood ignored the truth. He is not there to
    attack yet. He is there to pull the player toward the story and judge whether
    they will look away too.

Symbolic Reason
---------------
A lit house normally means safety.

In horror, corrupting something safe is powerful.

So this scene tells the player:

- Safety is fake here.
- Home is not safe.
- Warm light does not mean comfort.
- The neighborhood is wrong from the beginning.

This is why the silhouette should be inside the warm lit room instead of outside
in the street.

The house should feel normal first, then wrong.

Exact Player Experience
-----------------------
The scene should play like this:

1. Player enters the street.
   - It is raining.
   - The neighborhood is dark.
   - Most houses are dead and unlit.

2. One house has a porch light or warm window light.
   - This quietly tells the player: "go here."

3. As the player approaches, they pass a front or side window.
   - Inside, the room looks lived-in.
   - There is a lamp, a chair, maybe curtains, maybe faint TV static.

4. The player notices a figure inside.
   - At first, it reads like a tall person standing in the room.
   - It is not moving.
   - It is fully backlit, so the player only sees the outline.

5. On the second glance, the body feels wrong.
   - The shoulders are too broad.
   - The hood/head shape is not normal.
   - The arms hang too low.
   - The cloth hangs in an unnatural way.
   - The head may be slightly tilted too far.

6. A small flicker or lightning flash happens.
   - Not a loud jumpscare.
   - Just enough to sharpen the outline for a split second.

7. The player looks again.
   - The figure is gone.

8. When the player enters the house later, nobody is there.
   - This makes the player doubt what they saw.
   - It also makes the house feel haunted without needing a full enemy encounter.

The First Read / Second Read / Third Read
-----------------------------------------
This moment should be designed around three reads.

First Read:

    "Someone is inside."

This happens because the silhouette is upright, human-height, and standing in a
normal room.

Second Read:

    "Something is wrong with them."

This happens because the proportions are slightly off.

Third Read:

    "That was Penance."

This may happen later, after the church door or basement chase, when the player
recognizes the same hood/shape.

That delayed recognition is important. The player should remember the silhouette
and think:

    "Wait... that was the thing from the house."

Best Visual Setup
-----------------
Use a backlit silhouette.

Inside the room:

- Warm lamp, ceiling light, or hallway light behind Penance
- Bright enough to outline him
- Not so bright that the whole room looks overexposed

Outside the room:

- Dark rainy street
- Player mostly in darkness
- Window frame visible
- Light spilling out slightly

Penance:

- Body almost completely black
- Face not visible
- No front lighting
- Only the outline reads

The visual should be:

    bright background + dark figure + readable contour

Best Pose
---------
Use a still pose.

Recommended pose:

- Standing in profile or 3/4 angle
- Head slightly lowered or slightly tilted
- Shoulders broad
- Arms hanging low
- Cloth hanging downward
- No sudden movement

Why stillness matters:

If Penance waves, lunges, or moves too much, the player instantly reads him as a
monster.

If he stands still, the player first reads him as a person.
Then the fear comes from the question:

    "Why is he standing like that?"

Wrong Details to Add
--------------------
Do not show full monster detail yet.
Only add one or two wrong details.

Good wrong details:

- Slightly too tall
- Shoulders too wide
- Hood/head shape not normal
- Arms hanging too long
- Head tilted too far
- Cloth hanging where normal clothing should not
- Chain outline visible for one frame or one angle

Avoid:

- Full face reveal
- Obvious claws
- Huge monster proportions
- Weapon reveal
- Running animation
- Screaming jumpscare

The player should not think:

    "Monster enemy detected."

They should think:

    "Was that a person? No... wait... what was that?"

Set Dressing Around the Silhouette
----------------------------------
Add normal life details inside the room so the player initially accepts the space
as a real home.

Good details:

- Lamp on
- Chair near window
- Small table
- Curtains
- Faint TV static
- Family photo frames, but hard to read
- Old wallpaper
- Slight dust in the light

Why this helps:

Normal objects make Penance feel like he almost belongs there.
Then the player realizes he does not.

That contrast creates dread.

Audio Direction
---------------
Keep this moment quiet.

Use:

- Rain outside
- Faint indoor hum
- Maybe faint TV static
- One tiny chain creak
- Very soft low bass sting when the player notices the shape

Do NOT use:

- Loud scream
- Big orchestra hit
- Chase music
- Monster roar

This is not a jumpscare.
This is a "did I just see that?" moment.

Recommended Timing
------------------
Use a short reveal.

Suggested timing:

0.0s  Player enters window approach trigger.
0.2s  Interior light subtly flickers or brightens.
0.5s  Penance silhouette is visible.
1.5s  Optional lightning flash / tiny flicker.
1.8s  Penance disappears.
2.0s  Room returns to normal.

Total visible time:

    1.0 to 2.0 seconds

Do not leave him visible too long.
The player should have just enough time to question it, not enough time to study
it comfortably.

Godot Implementation Concept
----------------------------
Use an Area3D trigger outside the window.

Trigger conditions:

- Player enters window approach zone
- Event has not already played
- Player is roughly facing the window

Effect:

- Show Penance silhouette mesh
- Hold for about 1.5 seconds
- Flicker room light once
- Hide Penance silhouette
- Optionally play faint chain creak

Important:

Only trigger this once.
If repeated, it becomes predictable and loses power.

Simple Pseudocode
-----------------

var house_silhouette_seen = false

func _on_window_approach_trigger_entered():
    if house_silhouette_seen:
        return

    if player_is_facing_window():
        house_silhouette_seen = true
        play_house_silhouette_event()

func play_house_silhouette_event():
    show_penance_silhouette()
    play_faint_chain_creak()
    flicker_house_light_once()
    await get_tree().create_timer(1.5).timeout
    hide_penance_silhouette()

Practical Godot Node Setup
--------------------------
Suggested node structure:

LitHouse
├── WindowRevealTrigger (Area3D)
├── InteriorRoomLight (OmniLight3D or SpotLight3D)
├── WindowFrame / Glass
├── PenanceSilhouetteMarker (Marker3D)
├── PenanceSilhouetteMesh (hidden by default)
├── LightFlickerAnimation (AnimationPlayer)
└── HouseRevealAudio (AudioStreamPlayer3D)

PenanceSilhouetteMesh should be simple at this stage.
It does not need full detail because the player only sees the outline.

Use a dark material:

- Almost black albedo
- Low roughness variation if needed
- No bright front detail
- Shape readability matters more than texture

How to Prevent the Player Missing It
------------------------------------
Do not force a cutscene, but guide attention.

Use one or more of these:

- The window is on the natural path to the front door.
- The light flickers exactly when the player is near.
- A faint chain creak comes from inside the room.
- The porch light leads the player's eye toward the window.
- The window frame creates a clear view of the silhouette.

Optional safety method:

If the player enters the trigger but is not facing the window, delay the event
until they look close enough toward it.

Do not spin the camera hard.
That feels cheap.

What Happens After the Silhouette
---------------------------------
After the figure disappears, the player should still enter the house.

Do not immediately attack them.

Instead:

- The room is empty.
- The lamp is still on.
- Maybe there is a note or clue.
- Maybe the TV is still static.
- Maybe there is a faint mark or disturbed object where Penance stood.

This keeps the fear psychological.

The player should feel:

    "I know I saw something. So why is there nothing here?"

Connection to Later Game Beats
------------------------------
This first silhouette should echo later scenes.

Progression:

1. Lit house silhouette
   - Player only questions what they saw.

2. Distant sightings / shadows
   - Player starts recognizing the shape.

3. Cul-de-sac church door opening
   - Penance clearly interacts with the world.

4. Church interior tension
   - Penance feels closer.

5. First basement jumpscare/chase
   - Penance becomes an active threat.

6. Second basement stalking
   - Penance becomes a hunter.

7. Post-gun encounter
   - Penance proves the gun does not fully solve the problem.

This pacing prevents the monster from being overexposed too early.

Final House Scene Rule
----------------------
For the first lit-house sighting, remember:

    quiet > loud
    silhouette > detail
    doubt > explanation
    stillness > movement
    wrong proportions > obvious monster design

One-Line Version
----------------
The player should think someone is inside the lit house, then slowly realize the
shape is too still, too wrong, and too impossible to be human.

10. CUL-DE-SAC CHURCH EVENT
================================================================================

Main Setpiece Goal
------------------
At the cul-de-sac point:

- The player should feel lost because they reached a dead end.
- The player should turn around and go backwards.
- When the player goes back, Penance opens the church door ominously.
- The church door was previously closed and inactive.
- The player must have already walked past the church earlier.
- The player must reach close to the end of the cul-de-sac first.
- Only after turning back should the church door event happen.
- A scary/ominous light should appear at the church door.

Player Experience
-----------------
The intended emotional sequence:

1. Player walks into the cul-de-sac.

4. Player thinks: "I guess I go back..."

6. Something feels off.
7. Player looks toward the church.
8. Penance is there.
9. Penance slowly opens the door.
10. Penance did not exist there before.
11. Light spills out from the doorway.
12. Player thinks: "I have to go there."

13. PENANCE CHURCH EVENT DETAILS
================================================================================

This should be a fake cutscene.
It happens in-game, not as a full cinematic.

Event Sequence
--------------

1. Spawn Penance.
2. Slow or briefly lock the player.
3. Play the church door animation.
4. Turn on the doorway light.
5. Play audio layers.
6. Let Penance react.
7. Unlock the church.

Step 1: Spawn Penance
---------------------
Position:
- At the church door.

Rotation:
- Facing the door at first.
- Not facing the player initially.

Animation:

    idle -> reach -> open door

Step 2: Slow or Lock Player Movement
------------------------------------
Optional, but useful.

Options:
- Disable input for about 2 seconds.
- Or heavily reduce player movement speed.

Purpose:
- Forces the player to notice the door opening.
- Makes the moment feel cinematic without needing a cutscene.

Step 3: Door Animation
----------------------
Timeline:

    0.0s -> door starts closed
    1.0s -> door begins slow creak open
    3.0s -> door fully open

Add:
- Creaking wood sound
- Chain drag
- Low bass rumble

Step 4: Light Turns On
----------------------
This is critical.
The light is what guides the player.

Timing:
- Turn on the light at the same time the door starts opening.

Look:
- Strong warm/orange light from inside the church
- Fog or light spill outward
- Slight flicker

Visual Idea:
- The church should feel like a mouth opening.

Step 5: Penance Behavior After Door Opens
-----------------------------------------
Option A — Better Horror:
- Penance stands still for 1 to 2 seconds.
- Penance slowly turns head toward the player.
- Penance disappears.

Option B:
- Penance walks inside slowly.
- Penance vanishes in darkness.

Recommended:
- Use Option A.
- It is creepier because the player feels noticed.

23. OUTLAST-STYLE STEALTH / CHASE LOOP
================================================================================

Design Direction
----------------
Use an Outlast-style chase and stealth loop, but keep the AI simple and believable.
Do not over-engineer the monster.

Core rule:
- If Penance sees the player, Penance chases.
- If the player cuts line of sight, Penance does not instantly know their exact
  location forever.
- After losing line of sight, Penance searches based on the last known position
  and any noises the player makes.

Important Game Phase Rule
-------------------------
The game stays Outlast-style until the player acquires a gun.

Before gun:
- No real combat.
- Player survives by running, hiding, crouching, breaking line of sight, and
  managing stamina.
- Fear comes from being helpless.

After gun:
- The game can shift into a survival/action hybrid.
- The gun should not instantly remove fear.
- Recommended: gun slows, stuns, interrupts, or delays Penance rather than fully
  killing him easily.

================================================================================
24. PENANCE AI STATE MACHINE
================================================================================

Use a finite state machine.
Keep it clean:

    IDLE -> PATROL/STALK -> CHASE -> SEARCH -> STALK/CHASE

Recommended States
------------------

1. IDLE
   - Penance is inactive or hidden.
   - Used before the player triggers major horror sequences.

2. PATROL / STALK
   - Penance moves around the environment using interest points.
   - This should feel less like a guard route and more like a haunting presence.

3. CHASE
   - Penance has confirmed line of sight on the player.
   - Penance runs directly toward the player.

4. SEARCH
   - Penance lost line of sight.
   - Penance moves toward the last known player position.
   - Penance listens for noise.
   - If the player makes noise, Penance updates its target.

CHASE State
-----------
Condition:

    player is inside vision cone
    AND player is within detection range
    AND no wall blocks line of sight

Behavior:
- Run directly at the player.
- Play aggressive audio.
- Increase tension.
- Update last_seen_position while the player is visible.

LOST LINE OF SIGHT TIMER
------------------------
As soon as the player breaks line of sight:

Full game:

    los_timer_max = 3.5 seconds

Demo:

    los_timer_max = 2.5 seconds

Rules:
- If the player reappears before the timer ends -> return to CHASE.
- If the timer finishes -> enter SEARCH.

SEARCH State
------------
Search is what makes Penance feel smart without cheating.

Behavior:
- Move to last known player position.
- Look around.
- Move slower than chase speed.
- React to sound.
- Wander slightly around the last known position.
- If the player makes a loud noise, update the target position.

The player should feel:

    "It lost me, but it is still looking."

25. LINE OF SIGHT SYSTEM
================================================================================

Use two checks:

1. Vision cone / angle check.
2. Raycast check from Penance to player.

Detection Conditions
--------------------
Penance detects the player when:

    distance_to_player < detection_range
    AND player is inside vision_angle
    AND raycast from Penance to player is not blocked by wall

Suggested Detection Values
--------------------------

Full game:
- Detection range: 12-16 meters
- Vision angle: 90-110 degrees
- Too-close instant detect: under 3 meters

Demo:
- Detection range: 10-13 meters
- Recommended demo value: 12 meters
- Vision angle can stay the same
- Too-close instant detect can stay under 3 meters

Important Rule
--------------
Penance should not always know the player's exact position.
Use:
- Current position only when player is visible.
- Last seen position when line of sight is broken.
- Noise position when player makes sound.

Avoid:
- Enemy always tracking player perfectly through walls.
- Enemy vision being so wide that players feel helpless.

Reminder
--------
Add player implementation for:
- Crouching
- Running/sprinting
- Sprint stamina bar

Core Player Speeds
------------------
Use real-world metric scale:

    1 Godot unit = 1 meter

Lock these values for both the demo and the full game.

Player Speeds
-------------

Walk:

    2.5 m/s

Feel:
- Cautious exploration.
- Realistic enough for horror pacing.

Sprint:

    5.5 m/s

Feel:
- Panic run.
- Fast enough to escape if the player manages stamina well.

Crouch:

    1.4 m/s

Feel:
- Slow and stealthy.
- Slow enough to matter, but not so slow that it becomes annoying.

Stamina System

    5 seconds of full sprint

Drain rate:

    20 stamina per second

Regen delay:

Regen rate:

    12 stamina per second

When stamina is below 20%:

- Add slight camera shake.
- Reduce sprint speed from 5.5 m/s to about 4.8 m/s.

When standing/walking:
- Movement speed is 2.5 m/s.
- Noise is low to medium.

When sprinting:
- Movement speed is 5.5 m/s.
- Noise is high.

    Sprint = fast but loud.
    Crouch = slow but quiet.

28. ENEMY SPEED TUNING
================================================================================

Keep these speeds locked for both demo and full game.
Do not change the enemy/player core speed relationship later.

Enemy Speeds
------------

Patrol/Search:

    2.2 m/s

Purpose:
- Slower than player walking.
- Lets careful players reposition.
- Creates tension without feeling unfair.

Chase:

    4.8 m/s

Purpose:
- Slower than player sprint.
- Faster than player walk/crouch.

Aggro Burst, Optional:

    5.6 m/s for 1-2 seconds

Trigger:
- When Penance is very close, around under 4 meters.

Purpose:

------------------

Player sprint:

Enemy chase:

    4.8 m/s

Meaning:
- Player can escape if they use stamina well.

Important Balance Rule
----------------------
The player should escape by decision-making, not just raw speed.

If players say:
- "I got caught instantly" -> enemy is too fast or detection is too harsh.
- "I always escape easily" -> enemy is too slow or search is too weak.
- "I do not need crouch" -> noise system is broken or too forgiving.

30. CHASE FLOW EXAMPLE
================================================================================

Basement Chase Flow
-------------------

1. Jumpscare hits.
2. Player turns around.
3. Penance is behind them.
4. CHASE begins.
5. Player sprints.
6. Sprinting creates loud noise.
7. Penance follows.
8. Player turns a corner and breaks line of sight.
9. LOS timer starts.
10. If player keeps sprinting, noise helps Penance reacquire.
11. If player crouches or hides, Penance enters SEARCH.
12. Penance moves to last known position.
13. Penance listens.
14. If player makes noise, Penance moves toward the noise.
15. If Penance sees player again, CHASE restarts.

Player Choices During Chase
---------------------------

Sprint straight:
- Fast, but loud.
- Good for gaining distance.
- Bad if the player never hides.

Crouch in corner:
- Slow, but quiet.
- Good after breaking line of sight.
- Bad if Penance is already too close.

Hide behind object:
- Breaks line of sight.
- Works best if the player stops moving or crouches.

Stop moving:
- High survival chance if line of sight is already broken.
- Dangerous if Penance is close or the player is in open space.

Target Feel
-----------
The player should be able to escape without simply outrunning Penance forever.
Escape should come from:
- Breaking line of sight.
- Stopping noise.
- Crouching.
- Hiding.
- Managing stamina.

31. SIMPLE AI PSEUDOCODE
================================================================================

enum State { IDLE, STALK, CHASE, SEARCH }

var state = IDLE
var last_seen_position
var noise_target_position
var los_timer = 0.0
var los_timer_max = 2.5 # demo value; use 3.5 for full game

func update(delta):
    if can_see_player():
        state = CHASE
        last_seen_position = player.position
        los_timer = 0.0

    elif state == CHASE:
        los_timer += delta
        if los_timer > los_timer_max:
            state = SEARCH

    if state == CHASE:
        move_toward(player.position, chase_speed)

    elif state == SEARCH:
        move_toward(last_seen_position, search_speed)
        listen_for_noise()

    elif state == STALK:
        stalk_near_player()

func on_player_noise(noise_position, noise_radius):
    if distance_to(noise_position) <= noise_radius:
        noise_target_position = noise_position
        if state == SEARCH or state == STALK:
            move_toward(noise_target_position, search_speed)

34. THREE MODES OF PENANCE PRESENCE
================================================================================

1. DORMANT
----------
Before the player triggers major fear, Penance is mostly not physically present.

Use implied presence:
- Distant silhouettes.
- Lightning flashes.
- Reflections.
- Shadows.
- Sounds with no visible source.

Purpose:
- Builds fear before real AI appears.
- Makes Penance feel mythic and rare.

2. STALKING
-----------
This is the most important mode, especially during the second basement visit.

Penance moves between invisible interest points instead of fixed patrol routes.

Interest Point Examples:
- Hallway corners.
- Doorways.
- End of corridors.
- Behind obstacles.
- Intersections.
- Near objectives.
- Near the security room.

Behavior:
- Pick a nearby interest point.
- Move there quietly.
- Pause.
- Maybe appear briefly.
- Despawn, relocate, or move again.

The player should feel:

    "It is not chasing me right now...
     but it is definitely here."

3. CHASING
----------
Chase begins when:
- Penance confirms line of sight.
- Or the player makes loud noise nearby.

Then:

    STALKING -> CHASE -> SEARCH -> STALKING or CHASE

1. GAME-BREAKING / IMMERSION-BREAKING ISSUES — TOP PRIORITY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These matter more than extra scares, lore, models, or new mechanics.

Check for:

- Player gets stuck on geometry.
- Player clips into props, walls, stairs, fences, or doorframes.

- Chase path can be broken.
- Player can escape the scripted chase route.
- Player can miss the key and get softlocked.
- Player can miss the gun and get softlocked.
- Player can trigger events out of order.
- Player can leave an area before the event finishes and break progression.

Most Important Rule
-------------------

The player should ALWAYS be able to finish the demo without confusion or bugs.

A scary demo with one softlock feels worse than a simpler demo that works cleanly.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PLAYER ALWAYS KNOWS WHAT TO DO NEXT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The game should not use obvious UI arrows, but the player still needs direction.

At every moment, ask:

    "If I was a random player, would I know where to go?"

The player should feel like they discovered the next path naturally.
They should not feel like they are wandering randomly.

Use soft guidance:

Lighting
--------

- Bright area = go here.
- Flickering light = important.
- Ominous light = danger/objective.
- Darkness = not yet / unsafe / blocked.

Sound
-----

- Noise from a direction pulls the player forward.
- Bell, chain, whisper, TV static, rain shift, or door creak can guide attention.

Framing
-------

- Put important doors/windows in the player's natural view.
- Center the church door after the cul-de-sac return.
- Frame the security room clearly when the player gets close.

Motion
------

- Flicker lights.
- Move curtains.
- Swing objects.
- Let a door slowly open.

Good Demo Feeling
-----------------

The player should think:

    "I think I should go there."

Not:

    "Where the hell am I supposed to go?"

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. PACING — THIS IS HUGE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The demo pacing should feel like:

    Calm -> Weird -> Uneasy -> Scared -> PANIC -> Relief -> Scared again -> End

This pacing is more important than adding more monsters or more mechanics.

Common Mistakes
---------------

Too much nothing:
- Player walks for too long with no change.
- No sound cue.
- No visual event.
- No objective feeling.

Too many scares too fast:
- Player gets numb.
- Penance stops feeling special.
- The demo becomes noisy instead of scary.

How to Fix Slow Sections
------------------------

If a section feels boring, add one small atmospheric signal:

- sound cue
- tiny movement
- light flicker
- environmental change
- shadow crossing a window
- distant bell
- door creak
- monitor flicker

Do NOT always add a jumpscare.
Small tension is often better.

How to Fix Overly Intense Sections
----------------------------------

If the player is overwhelmed, add breathing space:

- quiet hallway
- slower walk moment
- temporary safety
- readable objective
- short pause before the next event

Important Rule
--------------

Scares need quiet space around them.
If everything is intense, nothing feels intense.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. VISIBILITY / DARKNESS BALANCE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This can ruin horror demos if tuned badly.

Bad Darkness
------------

- Player sees nothing.
- Player gets lost.
- Player misses doors.
- Player walks into walls.
- Player thinks the game is broken.

Bad Brightness
--------------

- Player sees everything.
- Penance loses mystery.
- The environment stops feeling dangerous.

Good Horror Visibility
----------------------

The player should see just enough.

Test these questions:

- Can the player clearly see doorways?
- Can the player barely see down long hallways?
- Does the flashlight feel useful?
- Is the flashlight not too overpowered?
- Can the player read the church entrance?
- Can the player identify the basement exit/security room?
- Can the player see enough during the chase to react?

Lighting Goal
-------------

Make darkness scary, not annoying.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. AUDIO CONSISTENCY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Audio sells the whole demo.
A simple scene with strong audio feels polished.
A detailed scene with broken audio feels cheap.

Make sure:

- Footsteps always play.
- Rain is consistent.
- Indoor/outdoor audio transitions are smooth.
- Penance sounds feel directional.
- Chase audio starts and ends cleanly.
- Gunshot sounds impactful.
- Door creaks and chain sounds are synced with animation.
- The bell sound is clear and memorable.
- No accidental silence happens.

Silence Rule
------------

Silence should feel intentional, not like a bug.

Good silence examples:

- right before Penance opens the church door
- right after the basement door closes
- right before the post-gun false relief scare
- right before the demo ending monitor flicker

Bad silence examples:

- footsteps stop for no reason
- rain cuts out randomly
- chase music disappears mid-chase
- ambience stops because of a trigger bug

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. PLAYER CONTROL FEEL
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

If movement feels bad, everything feels bad.

Check:

- Turning is not too slow.
- Turning is not too fast.
- Sprint responds instantly.
- Crouch feels intentional.
- Sprint bar/stamina feedback is readable.
- Low stamina breathing is clear.
- No input delay.
- Player does not slide too much.
- Player does not stop on tiny geometry.
- Stairs feel smooth.

Locked Movement Values
----------------------

Keep these consistent for demo and full game:

    Walk:   2.5 m/s
    Sprint: 5.5 m/s
    Crouch: 1.4 m/s

Do not change the core movement feel between demo and final.
Only change enemy perception/tuning if the demo needs to be easier.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. CAMERA FEEL
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Camera feel is subtle, but it affects whether the game feels polished.

Recommended Camera Additions
----------------------------

- Slight head bob while walking.
- Stronger but still controlled movement while sprinting.
- Small camera shake on major events.
- Slight camera reaction during door slam/door close/gunshot.
- Tighter camera feel in narrow basement/crawl spaces.

Avoid:

- excessive head bob
- motion sickness
- huge random camera shake
- forced camera movement too often
- taking control away from the player for too long

Rule
----

Use camera effects to support fear, not fight the player.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. FAIL + RETRY FLOW
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Players will get caught.
That is fine.
The retry flow must be clean.

Make sure:

- Death/failure is clear.
- Player understands they were caught.
- Restart is fast.
- No long reload screens.
- The game resets triggers correctly.
- Penance does not remain in a broken state after retry.
- Doors reset correctly.
- Key/gun states reset correctly if needed.
- Chase music stops and restarts correctly.

Good Retry Feeling
------------------

The player should think:

    "I messed up. I want to try again."

Not:

    "The game broke."

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. DEMO ENDING CLARITY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The player should KNOW the demo is finished.

Do not leave them wandering after the final scare.

Recommended Ending
------------------

After the player escapes the basement and sees the security monitor/TV:

- monitor flickers
- Penance appears somewhere impossible
- Penance turns slightly toward the camera
- screen glitches
- cut to black
- title card appears

Ending Text Options
-------------------

Option A:

    PENANCE
    This was only the first descent.

Option B:

    DEMO COMPLETE
    PENANCE

Option C:

    PENANCE
    The door was never open for you.

Best Pick
---------

Use a strong title card instead of overexplaining.
The demo should end with mystery, but the player should still understand the demo is over.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. PLAYTEST WITH SOMEONE WHO KNOWS NOTHING
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is one of the most important steps.

Do this:

- Let someone play without explaining anything.
- Do not guide them.
- Do not tell them where to go.
- Do not explain the mechanics.
- Watch what they naturally do.

Look for:

- where they get lost
- where they get bored
- where they get scared
- where they break the system
- where they miss important cues
- where they misunderstand objectives
- where they do something unexpected

Playtest Rule
-------------

If one player gets confused, watch carefully.
If multiple players get confused in the same spot, the design is the problem.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. THINGS NOT TO WORRY ABOUT YET
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Do NOT waste demo time on:

- perfect AAA textures
- full lore system
- multiple endings
- advanced UI
- full save system
- full combat polish
- extra enemies
- huge open map
- too many explorable houses
- complicated inventory
- advanced enemy AI everywhere
- full final-game balance

The demo does not need to prove every feature.
The demo needs to prove the core experience.

Core Demo Promise
-----------------

The player should leave thinking:

    "That was scary, polished, and I want to know what happens next."

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. FINAL PRE-DEMO CHECKLIST
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Before calling the demo done, verify:

[ ] Player never gets stuck.
[ ] Player always knows where to go.
[ ] All triggers fire correctly.
[ ] No trigger fires twice unless intended.
[ ] Penance never spawns awkwardly.
[ ] First lit-house silhouette works.
[ ] Cul-de-sac dead-end flow works.
[ ] Church door opening setpiece works.
[ ] First basement scripted chase works 100% of the time.
[ ] Second basement stalking moment works.
[ ] Basement door close moment works.
[ ] Key pickup cannot be missed in a softlocking way.
[ ] Gun reveal is clear.
[ ] Gun pickup works.
[ ] Gun shot reaction works.
[ ] Post-gun scare works.
[ ] Audio is consistent.
[ ] Lighting guides the player.
[ ] Darkness is scary but playable.
[ ] Restart after death works.
[ ] Ending hits clearly.
[ ] Title card/demo end is obvious.

Honest Take
-----------

You are not missing features anymore.

At this point, demo success depends on:

    polish, pacing, and reliability — not more ideas.

A smaller demo that works perfectly is better than a bigger demo that breaks.
